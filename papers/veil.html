Can You Tell a Smile from a Veil? How Buyers Respond When AI Rewrites Product Descriptions

Author: Haoran Zheng
Date: November 2026 (working paper)
Download PDF

Abstract

More and more marketplaces give sellers the option of having AI rewrite their product descriptions. A description carries two kinds of content: informative content that a buyer can check and persuasive content meant to win the buyer over. Persuasive content used to cost sellers time, so it told buyers something about the item; AI makes it almost free for every seller. How do buyers respond when AI rewrites the descriptions they read? We study Municibid, an online auction platform on which U.S. government agencies sell surplus vehicles and equipment. In January 2025, Municibid gave its sellers an AI writing assistant. First, in a controlled experiment, three large language models (LLMs) rewrote listings from before ChatGPT. They kept nearly all the facts and added persuasive content. However, when asked to make a listing appealing, the LLMs also added unsupported claims and made stated problems harder to see at first glance. Second, in the actual market, listings that a detector flagged as AI-written showed no detectable difference in page views, buyer questions to sellers or bids from the same seller’s human-written listings, and those questions almost never mentioned AI. Overall, we found that buyers did not turn away from AI-written listings: 86% of human-written listings received a bid, and AI-written ones by the same sellers did so 1.2 percentage points more often (95% CI [−4.9, +7.4]).
